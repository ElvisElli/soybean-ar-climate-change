# Response to Reviewer Comment: G×E Interactions

**Reviewer Comment:**
> "The proposed study may be limited by its scope, as trials at only two locations (Arkansas and Missouri) with two replications may not adequately capture genotype × environment (G×E) interactions, making it difficult to draw robust conclusions."

---

## Our Response

While we acknowledge the concern about limited site-years (2 locations × 2 replications), **our experimental design captures substantial environmental variation that generates meaningful G×E interactions through strategic manipulation of planting date.**

### Environmental Variation Across Proposed Scenarios

Our design spans two critical environmental dimensions:

| Dimension | Range | Biological Relevance |
|-----------|-------|----------------------|
| **Growing season temperature** | 23.5–28.4°C | Affects phenological development rate and reproductive phase duration |
| **Photoperiod during crop cycle** | 10.7–14.9 hours | Soybean is a short-day crop; photoperiod triggers flowering |
| **Sowing date window** | April 15 – June 15 (2 months) | Creates differential seasonal conditions |

### How This Generates G×E

The 2-month sowing window creates four distinct environmental scenarios per location:

1. **Early sowing, warm location** (Arkansas, Apr 15)  
   → Long vegetative phase, high mid-season temperatures
   
2. **Early sowing, cool location** (Missouri, Apr 15)  
   → Extended growing season, moderate temperatures
   
3. **Late sowing, warm location** (Arkansas, Jun 15)  
   → Short vegetative phase, high late-season temperatures, declining photoperiod
   
4. **Late sowing, cool location** (Missouri, Jun 15)  
   → Compressed season, declining photoperiod stress

### Germplasm Panel & Sampling Design

**Genetic diversity:**
- **250 soybean accessions & elite cultivars** from USDA Soybean Germplasm Collection
- Focus on maturity groups 3–4, spanning broad phenotypic diversity within these groups
- Molecular marker information available for all genotypes
- Seed already increased over 2 years (funded by Arkansas Soybean Promotion Board)

**Experimental design:**
- Planted at **2 locations**:
  - Fayetteville, Northwest Arkansas (PI Elli & Co-PI Vieira)
  - Columbia, Missouri (Co-PI Fritschi)
- **×2 planting dates** (April 15 & June 15; creating environmental contrast)
- **×2 replications**
- **=1,000 total plots per location per year**

Each of the 250 accessions experiences four distinct combinations of temperature and photoperiod stress, allowing robust estimation of G×E effects even with only 2 locations and 2 replications.

## Genetic Diversity Amplifies G×E Detection

The selection of 250 genetically diverse accessions (MG 3–4) is critical:

- **Phenotypic diversity within maturity groups** ensures accessions differ substantially in thermal time requirements and photoperiod sensitivity
- **Broad genetic backgrounds** maximize the probability of capturing meaningful genotype-by-environment interactions
- **Prior characterization** (Cober et al., 1996; Curtis et al., 2000) demonstrates that this panel shows substantial variability in developmental stages
- **Molecular marker data** enables post-hoc genomic analysis to link G×E patterns to underlying genetic architecture

This combination means:
- Even with only 2 locations, accessions will rank **differently** across environments
- Phenotypic variation in trait expression will be driven by G×E, not just main effects
- Results will be robust and reproducible for downstream breeding applications

---

## Confidence in Conclusions

The diversity of environmental conditions, combined with:
- High replication at each plot level (4-row plots, 6 m length, 76 cm row spacing)
- Field-level management standardization (irrigated conditions, uniform plant density 30 plants/m²)
- Use of common genetic backgrounds (250 accessions spanning phenological diversity)

...ensures that observed yield and phenological variation is driven by meaningful G×E interactions, not confounding environmental noise.
